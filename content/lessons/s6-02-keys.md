## A key is a family

Songs are usually in a **key**: a family of seven notes (a major scale) and the chords built on them. That's why some chords sound "right" together.

## The chords of C major

Build a chord on each note of the C major scale (C D E F G A B):

- **I** C · **ii** Dm · **iii** Em · **IV** F · **V** G · **vi** Am · (vii° B diminished, rarely used)

Uppercase = major, lowercase = minor. **I, IV and V** are the backbone of countless songs; **vi** adds a touch of sadness.

::chord C Dm Em F G Am

## Roman numerals travel

"I-vi-IV-V" means the same pattern in any key. In C: C-Am-F-G. In G: G-Em-C-D (your Campfire Four!).

::song circle-walk

## The capo

A capo moves every open shape up by the number of frets. Hotel California with capo 7 is the same trick: easy shapes, original key.

::tip Next time you meet a song, ask: which chord feels like home (I)? Which one pulls back to it (V)? That's ear training and theory at once.
