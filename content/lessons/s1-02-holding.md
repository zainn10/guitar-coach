## Why posture matters

How you sit decides how easy everything else is. A good position means your fingers reach the strings without strain, your wrist stays straight, and the guitar doesn't move while you play.

::posture

## The classical position (recommended for nylon guitar)

1. Sit on the **front half** of a chair without arms. Back straight but relaxed.
2. Put your **left foot on a footstool** (or a stack of books) about 15 cm high.
3. Rest the guitar's waist (the narrow middle) on your **left thigh**. The neck points up and to the left at about **45°**.
4. The back of the guitar leans lightly against your chest.
5. Your **right forearm** rests on the top edge of the body; your right hand hangs over the soundhole.

Check: let go with your left hand. The guitar should stay put.

## The casual position

Guitar on the **right leg**, neck roughly level. Fine for strumming songs, and many people find it comfortable at first. Try both; use the classical position for fingerpicking and barre chords later, because it gives your left hand the best reach.

::mistake wrist

::tip Whatever position you use, keep your shoulders down and relaxed. Tension in the shoulders travels into your hands.

::trouble guitar-slips hand-ache
