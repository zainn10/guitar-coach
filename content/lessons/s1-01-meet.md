## Your guitar

You have a **nylon-string guitar** (also called a classical or Spanish guitar). It's the instrument of classical and flamenco music, and it's the kindest guitar to learn on: the strings are soft on the fingertips and the sound is warm.

::guitar

- **Headstock and tuning pegs**: turn these to tune each string.
- **Nut**: the little bar at the top of the neck where the strings start.
- **Frets**: the metal wires across the neck. Pressing a string just behind a fret makes a higher note.
- **Soundhole and rosette**: where the sound comes out (the rosette is the decorative ring).
- **Bridge**: where the strings are tied on the body.

## The six strings

The strings are numbered **1 to 6, from the thinnest to the thickest**. When you hold the guitar, the thickest string (the 6th) is nearest your face.

Played open (with no fingers pressing), they are: **E A D G B E**, from thickest to thinnest. There are two E strings: a low one (6th) and a high one (1st).

::tip A memory aid: **E**very **A**ngry **D**og **G**rowls **B**arks **E**ats.

## How this course works

- **Small steps.** Every skill is broken into stages: one finger, then the shape, then the change.
- **Checks you must pass.** Each lesson ends with a short, measurable check. Pass it and the next lesson unlocks. You can always go back.
- **The app listens.** Many checks use the microphone: it tells you which string rang, which didn't, and whether you were on the beat.
- **Your weakest areas come first.** The Today page builds every practice session around what you find hardest, and brings back older skills just before you'd forget them.
- **Short is fine.** Five minutes a day beats an hour once a week.
