## Flamenco in a nutshell

Flamenco grew in Andalusia, in southern Spain, from a mix of Romani, Andalusian, Moorish and Jewish musical traditions. It has three pillars:

- **Cante**: the song, raw and expressive.
- **Baile**: the dance, with its footwork and handclaps.
- **Toque**: the guitar.

## How flamenco guitar differs

- **Rhythm is everything.** Each style (*palo*) has its own rhythmic cycle, the **compás**: some count in 4, some in 12 beats with accents in unexpected places.
- **Percussive techniques**: **rasgueado** (rapid fingers flicking across the strings), **golpe** (tapping the guitar's top), and fast **picado** runs.
- **A Phrygian sound**: much flamenco revolves around E, with the chord a fret above (F) pulling back to it. You'll play this in Stage 2.
- **The instrument**: flamenco guitars are lighter, with lower strings and a tap plate (*golpeador*). Your classical guitar works perfectly well for learning.

## What you'll learn here

E as home (Stage 2), the Andalusian cadence (Stage 3), the rumba strum and thumb (Stage 4), rasgueado (Stage 5), and a first falseta and the tangos rhythm (Stage 6). After that you can choose flamenco as your path.
